The Twin Vaults

      
Two linked vaults, cracked by two agents who can't communicate. With ordinary tools, their dials can agree at most 75% of the time — provably, mathematically, no exceptions. Equip them with entangled tools instead.

Each agent is given a random instruction. They must each answer without knowing the other's instruction, and they win when their answers satisfy a specific rule.

        
With ordinary (classical) tools, the best possible strategy wins exactly 75% of rounds — proven by the Bell/CHSH inequality, the same theorem physicists use to demonstrate entanglement is real.

        
Give both agents one half of an entangled pair, each measuring at a specific angle, and quantum mechanics predicts (2+√2)/4 ≈ 85.4% — genuinely above the classical ceiling. That's exactly what's simulated below.

The mission structure

    
    
      
  • Intro (interactive): tap boxes classically, trigger superposition, drag one tutorial block — gets hands dirty before the real game starts.
    • 
      
  • Level 1 — 4 boxes: one Search Step gives an exact, guaranteed 100% find. Best opening "wow" — it's not probabilistic here, it's certain.
    • 
      
  • Level 2 — 8 boxes: sweet spot at 2 steps (~94.5%); a 3rd step crashes it to ~33%. The single most quotable moment — encourage visitors to try the 3rd block deliberately.
    • 
      
  • Level 3 — 16 boxes: doubling the vault only costs one extra Search Step — the actual point of quantum speed-up, made visible.
    • 
      
  • Level 4 — Twin Vaults: the Bell/CHSH test, reskinned as entangled lockpicks beating a proven 75% classical ceiling.
    • 
      
  • Plenary: interactive case-file recap plus a personalised, copyable "mission report" — this is the shareable artefact visitors take away.
    • 
    


    
What this actually is

    
    
      
  • A classical computer (this device) simulating the real mathematics of quantum circuits and real quantum-mechanical predictions.
    • 
      
  • There is no physical quantum hardware involved — but every probability shown (100%, 94.5%, 96%, 75%, 85.4%) is genuine physics, not invented for effect.
    • 
    


    
If someone asks "is this a real quantum computer?"

    
Be straightforward: no, it's a simulation on ordinary hardware. What's real is the physics — the maths and statistics are exactly what a physical quantum computer would also produce.


    
Running it unattended

    
    
      
  • The stepper at the top lets a visitor jump back to a completed level if they want to replay it.
    • 
      
  • Levels unlock in order; "Continue" appears after their first run in each level, so nobody gets stuck.
    • 
      
  • No login, timer, or account — safe to leave running on a kiosk all day.
